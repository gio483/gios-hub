# Keylight

A physically accurate flash simulator for interior architectural photography.
Place lights on a floor plan, set the camera, fire the shutter, get scored
against an art director's rubric.

Static site. No build step, no backend, no accounts. Progress lives in
`localStorage`. Deployed at `/keylight/` on the hub.

## Why the physics are trustworthy

Everything is computed in real photometric units (cd/m2, lux, lux-seconds) and
normalised to a picture only at the very end, through one identity:

    R = Lt * ISO / (K * N^2)          K = 12.5, R = 1.0 is middle grey

Ambient's `Lt` contains shutter. Flash's does not. That single asymmetry - the
thing the whole app exists to teach - is structural in the code rather than a
special case, and it is what makes the sim safe to probe at the edges.

Nothing is a lookup table. The guide-number coefficient is forced by the
definition of a guide number. Bounce loss falls out of beam geometry and
surface albedo. The interior-to-window gap is never authored: only the exterior
EV is, and the room's level comes from the solid angle the window subtends.

Run the tests:

    node --no-warnings test/physics.test.mjs

67 assertions, including sunny 16, energy conservation of bounce, guide-number exposure, inverse square, the
sync-speed wall, mired-shifted gels, and the shutter/flash asymmetry.

## Layout

    src/physics/     headless, unit tested, no DOM and no renderer
    src/render/      three.js engine, shaders, scene compiler, mask pass
    src/scenes/      seven hand-authored rooms
    src/game/        rubric, diagnostics, learning modules, challenges
    src/ui/          floor plan, controls, histogram
    test/            zero-dependency test harness
    vendor/          three.js r169 (MIT), vendored so the app makes no
                     network request at runtime

## Rendering

Every surface is generated procedurally in the shader from world position -
plank floors with staggered boards, plaster, marble veining, tile and grout,
fabric weave, rug pile, book spines, brushed metal, leather - with
derivative-based bump mapping, so a phone preview and a 6000px capture read
the same material. Ambient occlusion is applied only to the interreflected
fill (direct light is already shadow-rayed), corners and contact shadows
fall out of the room geometry and the occluder boxes, and the capture's
accumulation passes carry sub-pixel jitter, which resolves geometric edges
for free. Baseboards, crown, window casings and sills are generated by the
scene compiler, not authored per room.

## Scoring is an incident meter

The rubric converts every pixel to what an 18% grey card would have read
under the same light (each material's albedo is known), centred on the
interior convention of the card sitting half a stop under. A walnut-panelled
study at a perfect exposure therefore scores as lit, not as underexposed.
Colour consistency measures the light, not the paint: pixels are divided by
their surface tint, and materials whose specular rivals their albedo are
excluded as unreliable witnesses. Region statistics are single-pass
histograms - scoring a two-megapixel frame costs about 150ms.

All eleven Learning Center modules are validated by scripted playthroughs
against the app's own goal evaluation.

## The two-tier render

`renderPreview` is ambient only, half resolution, one pass, and runs
continuously. Flash is invisible there because flash is invisible through a
viewfinder in real life.

`capture` is full resolution with progressive accumulation; each pass jitters
the sample point on every area source, so penumbra width comes out of real
source geometry rather than a softness slider. Pass count adapts to what the
hardware delivered last time.
