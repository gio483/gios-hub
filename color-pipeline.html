Color Pipeline

One grade, every camera. Set the camera right, normalise, then one LUT on top.

1The three stages

Stage 1

Normalise

Each camera's own transform into Rec.709. The only part that differs per body.

Stage 2

Match

Grey card at the head of every roll. Balance each camera to neutral.

Stage 3

Look

The Mint LUT. Identical on every clip from every camera.

A single LUT can't match six cameras on its own — they start from different colour science. Stage 1 is what makes stage 3 behave the same everywhere.

2On the day — camera settings

Do this on every camera

White balance
Manual 5600K outdoors. Never auto — auto WB is why multicam never matches.
Sharpening
Lowest setting. Our reference is less sharpened than our own footage, not more.
Resolution
4K, even when delivering 1080. Downscaling gives real sharpness with no halos.

3Calibration shoot done 9 Nov

Result — the three cameras now match

Shot on the SDLR polo in the backyard. Measured across the graded clips: blacks and whites land within 2/255 of each other, saturation within 0.017. Each body has its own normalise LUT; they all share one look.

Sony FX3
S-Log3 → official Sony transform → Mint Sky Safe
GoPro Hero 12
SVM Normalise — GoPro Hero12 Flat → Mint Sky Safe
Osmo Pocket 3
SVM Normalise — Osmo Pocket3 DLogM → Mint Sky Safe

What capped the accuracy — fix on the next one

Sun in frame
On both action cams. That makes veiling glare that varies across the frame — the GoPro reads the polo brighter than the fence while the FX3 reads it darker. No LUT undoes a spatial haze. Open shade or overcast.
No grey card
The match had to be built from surfaces found in frame instead.
Split timing
The Pocket 3 rolled 30 min after the others, so the sun had genuinely moved. Shoot every body back to back.

The protocol below still stands — a re-shoot in soft light with a card would tighten this further.

Change these two profiles first

Grey card, first 3 seconds, every take

An 18% grey card held in frame, lit by the same light as the scene. White foamcore works if that is what you have. This one thing is most of the value of the shoot — without it, neutral has to be estimated off concrete and paint lines.

Locked identical on every camera

White balance
A fixed Kelvin number — 5600 daylight, 6500 shade. One camera left on auto and the match is only valid for that clip.
Frame rate
The same on all of them — 24 or 30
Shutter
1/48 at 24 · 1/60 at 30
Sony ISO
800 — S-Log3 base on all three bodies
Exposure
Grey card landing in the same place on every camera

Open shade or overcast — not hard sun

The GoPro and Pocket 3 have fixed apertures and no internal ND. In bright sun they sit pinned overexposed and cannot be matched to the Sonys at base ISO — no amount of post fixes it. Soft light keeps all of them in range. Bring the Pocket 3 magnetic NDs if you have them.

One frame, everything in it

  • A person. Skin is the hardest match and the one that actually matters.
  • Deep shadow and a bright sky or white wall in the same frame — that is what shows how each camera's toe and shoulder behave.
  • Green plants plus grey concrete or stucco.
  • Same framing, same spot, every body back to back inside a few minutes so the sun does not drift.

Ten seconds each, whichever bodies you have on you that day:

▸Sony FX3 / FX30 / A7 IVS-Log3, ISO 800
▸DJI Osmo Pocket 3D-Log M
▸GoPro Hero 9 / 12Flat / GP-Log
▸iPhone 15 Pro MaxApple Log — closes the last gap

The iPhone path is the one part of this system still specified but never tested. It is a ten-second add while you are already standing there.

4In Resolve

Easiest: let Resolve normalise everything

Project Settings → Color Management → DaVinci YRGB Color Managed, output Rec.709 Gamma 2.4. Then set each clip's Input Color Space in the media pool. All six cameras land on one consistent base automatically — this is the whole reason colour management exists.

1Transform to Rec.709skip under RCM
2Balance — grey card, offset, exposureper clip
3Mint Look LUTSky Safe for SDLR
4Trim — contrast / sat to tastesmall
5Grain (optional)hides codec noise

Fix a warm or dark clip in node 2, not node 4. Trimming after the look fights the look.

LUTs live in Resolve under LUT / SVM /. Pick by how it was shot:

▸Mint Look Sky Safe — shot in log, normalisedthe default
▸Mint Look Baked Profile — standard profile, S-Cinetone, phonenot log
▸Mint Look — faithful to the referencerotates skies to cyan

Normalise LUTs, measured from the 9 Nov shoot. Use these on those two bodies instead of a transform node:

▸SVM Normalise — GoPro Hero12 Flat to Rec709then Mint
▸SVM Normalise — Osmo Pocket3 DLogM to Rec709then Mint

Using the log version on non-log footage stacks two contrast curves — shadows block up and mids go olive. That's what Baked Profile is for.

6The sharpness rules

The look we're chasing is not sharper than ours — it's cleaner. Measured at matched pixel scale it has 17% edge-halo artifacts against 41% in our own reel. Sharpness here is subtractive.

  • Sharpening down or off in every camera.
  • No sharpen / clarity / texture pass in post, ever.
  • Shoot 4K even for 1080 delivery.
  • Upload 4K to YouTube — the encode bitrate is far higher, so even 1080 viewers see a cleaner image.
  • Low ISO, correct exposure. Noise → NR → smearing → the urge to sharpen. That loop is what looks cheap.
