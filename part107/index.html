Reading procedure

Work the chart in this order

  1. Find the airport symbol and read its colour. Blue means an operating control tower, magenta means no control tower.
  2. Trace the boundary through your point. Solid blue is Class B, solid magenta is Class C, dashed blue is Class D, dashed magenta is a Class E surface area.
  3. Read the floor and ceiling figures. They are hundreds of feet MSL with the last two zeros dropped. SFC in the floor position means the airspace starts at the ground.
  4. Convert your own altitude. Field elevation plus 400 gives you the MSL number the chart speaks in.
  5. Check obstructions. The upper figure is the top in feet MSL, the figure in parentheses is the height above the ground.
  6. Read the MEF for the quadrangle. It is the highest terrain or obstruction in that box, in hundreds of feet MSL, rounded up.

Exam facts

Verified August 2026

Changing 27 October 2026

The FAA Airman Testing Community Advisory, June 2026 edition, states that from 27 October 2026 the UAG and Instrument Rating Airplane tests will include image based questions using images not found in the test supplements, sourced from current VFR and IFR low altitude charts. Memorising the figures in FAA-CT-8080-2H stops being a strategy on that date. The FAA has not yet set a date for removing the printed supplement from test centres.

Sources

Where the answers come from

Every question carries its citation. The primary sources used are 14 CFR Parts 3, 48, 89, 91 and 107 from the eCFR, the Aeronautical Chart Users Guide effective 9 July 2026, the Aviation Weather Handbook FAA-H-8083-28B, the Aeronautical Information Manual, AC 107-2A, the Remote Pilot Study Guide FAA-G-8082-22 and the Pilots Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge FAA-H-8083-25C.

FAA-G-8082-22 is still the August 2016 edition. It predates night operations without a waiver, operations over human beings and all of Part 89, so this app cites the regulation itself for those subjects.
